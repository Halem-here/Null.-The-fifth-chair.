# NULL — Working Story Bible

> This is the current story design for the project. There is no separate canon document being treated as authority; the game and this file are the working draft. The aim is a fair mystery about what people know, what they choose to repeat, and what the evidence cannot settle.

## The story in one paragraph

A year after five friends voted on what happened to Arlo Penn, an investigator is handed the file at the Hartwell house. In 2015, MIST capsized at Greywater. Dess sent Arlo’s mother “All safe.” from the beach; the first emergency call came 48 minutes later. In 2016, the friends’ vote was not unanimous, though they later described it that way. Nell asked them simply to say what happened, then wrote that she would not return to the table. Later, messages to her sister Wren appear to come from Nell, but they were sent using a prepaid SIM found in Livia’s coat. The investigator can establish several acts of evasion and care, but cannot prove who untied the rope, whether Arlo was alive when the text was sent, or where Nell is now.

## The player’s role and task

The player is an outside reader of the file, not one of the five friends. At the start they know the boat capsized, Arlo later drowned, the five friends held a vote, and Nell’s papers make the gathering feel unfinished. They are asked to read the evidence carefully, interview the group, sort each item as Fact / Told / Guessed / Struck, solve the timeline and sign-off questions, rule on claims as TRUE / FALSE / NULL, and give a three-part account to one recipient.

The central rule is: **unproven is not false**. The player is not expected to solve every blank. They are expected to distinguish a record from a person’s account, and a person’s account from a guess.

## What the player can establish

- A photograph records MIST capsized at 20:41. It does not identify the people in the water.
- Arlo was found drowned the next morning. The record gives no time of death.
- Dess’s phone sent “All safe.” from the Greywater beach cell sector at 21:52. The first call to the emergency line came at 22:40—48 minutes later. The file does not establish when a voice call first became possible, why the group waited, or whether the delay changed anything.
- Dess admits sending the message to stop Arlo’s mother calling. Teo says Dess wanted the group to agree on what to say before calling. These are significant choices, but they do not prove Dess caused Arlo’s death or untied the rope.
- The 2016 vote sheet records two TRUE votes, two NULL votes and one FALSE. The claim that it was unanimous NULL is false.
- Nell’s pages say she had packed and would not return to the table. They establish what she wrote and suggest an intention to leave; they do not establish where she went or whether she is alive.
- Wren received fourteen messages from a prepaid number, with a different sign-off from Nell’s. The SIM with that number was in Livia’s coat. Livia admits she used it to write as Nell. That establishes Livia’s deception, not Nell’s whereabouts or Livia’s knowledge of them.

## What remains open

- Who untied MIST’s rope, and whether anyone intended harm.
- Whether Arlo was alive at 21:52; whether an emergency voice call could have been made then; and whether the wait mattered.
- Whether the heavy object Teo heard was Nell’s bag.
- Whether Nell left, where she went, and whether she is alive.
- Whether Livia knows anything about Nell’s location.

The rope is always described as **untied**, not “cut”: those are different actions, and the draft has no evidence that the rope was cut. The forum post now uses the same word as the interviews and remains an unsupported rumour.

## The fifth chair

The five chairs are for the five friends—Dess, Corin, Teo, Livia and Nell. Nell once texted ahead to save the seat by the window. A year later, that place is empty. **The fifth chair is Nell’s place among the five, not an extra chair for Arlo.** It is an image of absence, not proof that Nell is dead, or proof of how she left. Endings keep the chair empty rather than inventing a sixth seat or claiming to know where she went.

## Timeline anchors

### 2015 — MIST
- About 19:15: MIST leaves the dock (Corin’s account).
- 20:41: Teo’s photograph records the capsized boat.
- 21:52: Dess’s phone sends “All safe.” from the beach cell sector.
- 22:40: First emergency call, from the Hartwell landline.
- 07:10 next morning: Arlo is found on the east shore; cause of death is drowning.

### October 2016 — the vote
- About 18:30: Nell arrives after texting to save her a seat by the window.
- 22:40: Vote on “What happened to Arlo was nobody’s fault”: Dess TRUE, Corin TRUE, Teo NULL, Livia NULL, Nell FALSE.
- 22:55: Dess’s notebook says the front door was bolted; it names no one as responsible.
- 00:10: Livia says she went to Nell’s door.
- 00:40: Teo says he heard a door and something heavy dragged downstairs; he thinks it was a bag.
- 01:15: A dock-camera still shows no person and no boat at that moment.

## Character voices

- **Dess:** controlled and practical; she edits herself, defends her choices, and admits only what the evidence forces into the room.
- **Corin:** careful and hedging; he repeats things he heard and is uncomfortable when asked what he verified.
- **Teo:** plainspoken, self-deprecating, patchy about sequence; he jokes or trails off, but does not deliver polished speeches.
- **Livia:** warm and attentive, often offers food or care to close a subject; when pressed, she is direct about what she did and what she does not know.
- **Wren:** tired, direct, and done with being managed. She wants honesty more than reassurance.
- **Nell:** heard through short notes and texts. She asks for one plain acknowledgment, not a culprit or a theory.

Dialogue should sound spoken, with contractions and unevenness. Characters should not explain facts they already know just for the investigator’s benefit. Avoid tidy therapeutic one-liners; let hesitation, defensiveness and small acts of care carry the emotion.

## Ending purpose

The endings differ in whom the investigator chooses to address and how faithfully they handle the evidence. The strongest evidence-led account names the message, the delay, the false claim of a unanimous vote, Livia’s proved impersonation, and the remaining unknowns. It does not name a rope culprit, decide Arlo’s time of death, or declare Nell dead or safe. The emotional answer to Nell’s request is simple: **it happened**.
